Cyclical Mental Health

Understanding our internal rhythms to live in balance

What is cyclical mental health?

Cyclical mental health refers to how our emotional and psychological well-being fluctuates in natural cycles. These cycles can be influenced by hormonal, biological, seasonal, or emotional factors. Recognizing these patterns allows us to live in harmony with our rhythms and take better care of our minds.

Cycles that influence mental health

• Menstrual cycle

During the different phases of the cycle, hormones affect mood, energy, and concentration. It's not weakness, but a natural response of the body.

• Sleep cycles

Sleeping well regulates neurotransmitters like serotonin and dopamine. Lack of rest can amplify negative emotions or symptoms of stress.

• Seasonal cycles

Sunlight influences the production of serotonin and melatonin. Cold months or those with little light can bring more tiredness or sadness.

• Emotional cycles

Our moods also follow patterns: days of introspection, expansion, or rest. Listening to these rhythms is key to maintaining balance.

• How to take care of your cyclical mental health

    •Track your cycles: use a journal or app to observe changes in your mood and energy.
    •Adapt your routines: don't demand the same from yourself every day; allow rest when your body asks for it.
    •Nourish body and mind: eat well, move, and get enough sleep.
    •Practice self-compassion: fluctuating is human; you're not "wrong" for having difficult days.
    •Seek professional support: if emotional changes affect you a lot, seek psychological help.
Remember: your mind, like the moon, has phases. Accepting and caring for them is a profound act of self-love.
